# Provenance receipts for AI-authored code (audit capsule + merge gate)

**Slug:** r2-ai-code-provenance-receipts  |  **Track:** B  |  **Researched:** 2026-08-30  |  **Status:** researched  |  **Origin:** round 2 (asset-suggested)

## One-line pitch
A GitHub Action that emits a signed, offline-verifiable receipt for every agent-authored PR — which agent, which model, which files were fenced, which checks ran, which human approved — plus a merge gate that blocks agent PRs without one, so a regulated team can hand an auditor proof instead of a screenshot.

## Specific buyer

**Title:** VP Engineering / Head of Platform, or the security-compliance owner (often a "Head of Security & Compliance" wearing the SOC 2 hat) at a 20-300 person company that is (a) SOC 2 Type II or ISO 27001 audited and (b) has just turned on Claude Code / Codex / Cursor agents org-wide.

**Segments named in the brief:** fintech, healthtech, defense, any SOC 2-audited B2B SaaS. Secondary buyers hypothesized in the brief: SOC 2 / ISO 27001 audit firms, and GRC platforms (Vanta, Drata).

**Verdict on the buyer after research:** the developer-side pain is real and loud. The *compliance-side* buyer that the brief hypothesizes could not be found saying anything. Specifically:

- I checked a real SOC 2 audit firm's public article index ([johansonllp.com/blog](https://www.johansonllp.com/blog), fetched 2026-08-30). Zero posts about AI-generated code, AI in the SDLC, or auditing AI coding tools. Their only AI content is ISO 42001 (AI *management systems*, i.e. companies that ship AI products — not companies whose devs use AI).
- Vanta's own AI product ([vanta.com/products/ai-governance](https://www.vanta.com/products/ai-governance), fetched 2026-08-30) is scoped to **agent inventory and access**, not code provenance: "Automatically identify agents across laptops, code, and agent harnesses", "Evaluate what each agent can reach—down to models, prompts, and database access". It is a **waitlist**, not a shipping product, and there is no pricing. If the GRC platforms thought "who wrote this change" was the AI question, they would be building that. They are building asset inventory instead.
- The regulatory forcing function the brief assumed does not exist. The EU's [Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-Generated Content](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/code-practice-ai-generated-content) became legally binding **2 August 2026** — four weeks ago — and its scope is "marking and detection of AI-generated content and labelling of deepfakes and certain AI-generated publications" for **audio, image, video, and text**. Fetched 2026-08-30: no mention of source code, programming, or software. EU AI Act Art. 50 does not reach the repo.

So the buyer who *feels* the pain is an engineering lead. The buyer the brief wants to sell to (compliance/auditor) is not asking yet, publicly, anywhere I could reach.

**Where they read:** Hacker News, GitHub issue threads on `anthropics/claude-code` and `openai/codex`, The New Stack, OpenSSF working groups (free and open — see Reachability), OWASP chapters, LinkedIn CISO orbit.

## Reachability (50 qualified buyers in 30 days, $0)

Reaching *developers* who feel this pain: easy. Reaching the *compliance owner who signs a $200-500/mo check*: hard for a junior with no security brand.

Channels that actually work at $0:

1. **OpenSSF working groups** — [openssf.org](https://openssf.org/community/openssf-working-groups/) states plainly: "OpenSSF Working Groups are open to everyone!" Every group has a public GitHub repo, Slack channel, and mailing list. The two that matter: **Supply Chain Integrity WG** (owns SLSA, GUAC, gittuf — "understand and make decisions on the provenance of the code they maintain, produce and use") and **AI/ML Security WG**. Participating in [ossf/tac#628](https://github.com/ossf/tac/issues/628) and [ossf/tac#605](https://github.com/ossf/tac/pull/605) puts Alex in a thread with David A. Wheeler and the OpenSSF TAC. This is genuine career capital and it costs nothing. It is **not** a sales channel.
2. **GitHub issue threads** — `openai/codex#28058`, `openai/codex#12190`, `ossf/tac#628` each have identified, named humans with the exact pain and a public handle. Maybe 20-40 such people across the repos I searched. Reachable by contributing to the thread, not by DM.
3. **Hacker News** — the graveyard below shows every Show HN in this category lands at 1-5 points. HN is reachable but does not convert here.
4. **Boston, in person** — Boston is dense in the nominal ICP (Toast, Klaviyo, Flywire, Wayfair, plus a long tail of Series A/B fintech and healthtech, plus the defense/GovCon belt around Hanscom and Cambridge). Alex can physically attend OWASP Boston, Boston DevSecOps, and Boston CISO-adjacent meetups for $0. **This is the only channel where a student can plausibly get in front of a compliance owner.**
5. **Cold outbound to auditors** — I found no evidence audit firms are asking about this. Cold email to auditors would be selling them a problem they have not told anyone they have.

**Honest assessment: 50 qualified buyers in 30 days at $0 is not achievable for this idea.** 50 *developers* who nod along, yes. 50 people with budget authority who agree AI code provenance is a line item, no — because the evidence says fewer than 50 such people exist and none of them have said so publicly.

## Wedge
The brief proposes a GitHub Action at $200-500/month per org, or a paid pilot with one Boston regulated startup.

The $200-500/mo Action is the weaker half: it is priced above CodeRabbit's per-seat economics for a 10-person team ($240-480/mo), while delivering something no one has budgeted for, against a free Apache-2.0 reference implementation and a standards body actively defining the format.

The **paid pilot with one Boston regulated startup** is the only version worth testing, and it is a services sale, not a product: "let me instrument your agent PRs for 6 weeks and hand your auditor a bound evidence pack." That is a $2-5K one-time engagement, track-B one-time revenue, and it does not compound into recurring revenue without a buyer who renews — which requires the auditor to actually ask for it next cycle.

Before building anything, the cheap falsifying test: get three SOC 2 auditors on a 15-minute call and ask "in your last ten audits, did you ask a client how they control AI-authored code changes under CC8.1?" If the answer is no three times, the idea is dead and it cost three calls.

## Unit economics

- **Recurring burn:** effectively $0. A GitHub Action runs on the customer's runners. A receipt registry could be a static S3/R2 bucket or GitHub Releases. Sigstore's public good instance is free. Comfortably under the $40/mo cap.
- **Price if the $200-500/mo Action worked:** 5 orgs at $300 = $1,500 MRR. That would be a real outcome.
- **Price reality check:** the comparable per-PR GitHub App (CodeRabbit) is $24-48/dev/mo. A 15-dev regulated startup pays CodeRabbit $360-720/mo for something developers ask for daily. Asking the same company for $300/mo for something *no developer asks for and no auditor requires* is a harder sale at a similar price.
- **Pilot economics:** $2-5K one-time per Boston pilot, maybe 2-3 achievable in a year given a student's access. $5-15K one-time, zero compounding, high effort per dollar.
- **CAC:** enterprise-shaped. Security/compliance buyers require a security review of *your* tool. A solo undergrad vendor asking a fintech to install a merge gate in their SDLC will be asked for a SOC 2 report of his own. This is a real, structural blocker, not a hypothetical.

## Risks

1. **The standard is being set for free, right now, by people with more standing.** OpenSSF TAC issue #628 (2026-06-30) and PR #605 (2026-04-28, "Add AI Contribution Policy for OpenSSF Technical Initiatives", still open) are actively converging on `Assisted-by: AGENT_NAME:MODEL_VERSION` plus a signed in-toto predicate. The Linux kernel already documents the trailer ([docs.kernel.org/process/coding-assistants.html](https://docs.kernel.org/process/coding-assistants.html)): "Assisted-by: AGENT_NAME:MODEL_VERSION [TOOL1] [TOOL2]", example "Assisted-by: Claude:claude-3-opus coccinelle sparse". Sigstore and SLSA are named as adopters. **Format ownership is gone.** What remains sellable is the SaaS around it, which is the part with no demonstrated buyer.
2. **The agent vendor already ships the evidence layer, free.** Claude Code's OpenTelemetry export ([code.claude.com/docs/en/monitoring-usage](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/monitoring-usage), fetched 2026-08-30) emits `claude_code.user_prompt`, `claude_code.assistant_response`, `claude_code.tool_decision`, `claude_code.tool_result`, and optionally `claude_code.api_request_body` / `api_response_body` (full conversation history), correlated by `prompt.id` — "To trace all activity triggered by a single prompt, filter your events by a specific `prompt.id`." Admins configure it org-wide via managed settings. No plan tier named. Anything Alex builds is a formatter on top of a free vendor feed, and the vendor is one release away from binding it to the PR itself.
3. **No regulatory forcing function.** EU AI Act Art. 50 / the Code of Practice on Transparency (binding 2026-08-02) covers audio, image, video, and text media — **not source code**. SOC 2 CC8.1 is a principles-based change-management control that a Vanta screenshot of a GitHub branch-protection rule already satisfies. Nothing forces this purchase on a date.
4. **The incumbents will bundle it.** Sonar gates "AI Code Assurance" at Enterprise. Codacy gates "AI Risk Hub" at Business and has already announced a forthcoming "AI BOM." Endor Labs sells "AI Coding Agent Governance." These are shipping features in existing sales motions with existing security reviews already passed.
5. **The graveyard is large and recent.** Every attempt in the last 12 months at exactly this landed at 1-5 HN points and <40 GitHub stars — and at least one author has already publicly withdrawn. `arian-gogani/nobulex` (39 stars) is now described by its own author as: "**This repository is a prior direction, kept rather than deleted.** ... Nobulex is now the independent reliability registry for agent tools ... Same name, different thing." (README, fetched 2026-08-30). He built "signed, offline-verifiable receipts for AI agent actions" and moved on to a different question.
6. **Attestation of process is attestable, not verifiable.** As `thillel` put it (HN 48199719): "a determined person can route all of that through an LLM too." A receipt says a human clicked approve. It cannot say a human understood. An auditor who thinks about this for ten minutes will notice, and the receipt's value collapses to what a GitHub branch-protection screenshot already provides.
7. **Buyer inaccessibility for this specific principal.** Security/compliance buyers procure from vendors who can survive a vendor security review. A solo student cannot.

## Kill criteria

Pre-committed, checkable, cheap:

1. **Auditor check (do this first, costs 3 calls).** Ask three SOC 2 / ISO 27001 auditors: "In your last ten audits, did you ask any client how they control or evidence AI-authored code changes?" If fewer than two say yes → **kill**. Current public evidence predicts zero yes.
2. **Buyer check.** 10 conversations with eng leads at 20-300 person regulated companies. If fewer than 3 say "yes, I would pay for this next quarter" *with a named budget line* → **kill**. Nodding is not a yes.
3. **Standard check.** If `ossf/tac#628` converges on a free reference implementation with any adoption before Alex ships → **kill**, or pivot to being a contributor for career capital only.
4. **Platform check.** If GitHub, GitLab, or Anthropic ships per-PR agent attribution as a native feature → **kill immediately**.
5. **Traction check.** If a public launch (GitHub marketplace + Show HN) gets under 100 stars and zero inbound from a regulated company in 60 days → **kill**. Base rate from the graveyard predicts ~5-40 stars.

## Verdict

**Kill as a standalone product. Harvest the pieces.**

The research supports the *premise* and refutes the *business*. Untraceable agent-authored code is a real, current, articulated problem — `eranation`'s comment is the most precise statement of it I have read anywhere, and OpenSSF opened a formal proposal about it eight weeks ago. But three findings, each independently sufficient, close the commercial door:

1. **The format is being standardized as free infrastructure right now.** OpenFab's `openfab/generation` in-toto predicate already specifies per-range human-vs-AI authorship, model, prompt fingerprint, embedded acceptance contract, and N-of-M human sign-offs — Apache-2.0, vendor-neutral, in an active OpenSSF TAC thread, with the Linux kernel's `Assisted-by:` trailer converging alongside it. Alex would be shipping a paid version of a free standard, four days behind its latest revision.
2. **The compliance demand the whole thesis rests on does not exist yet.** EU AI Act transparency covers media, not code. A real SOC 2 audit firm's public writing has nothing about AI in the SDLC. Vanta's AI product is agent inventory on a waitlist. Nine verbatim complaints, zero auditors.
3. **The market has already voted, repeatedly.** Every AI-code-governance launch in 2026 scored 1-5 HN points. The most on-the-nose one — literally "signed, offline-verifiable receipts for AI agent actions" — got 39 stars and its author publicly relabelled it "a prior direction, kept rather than deleted."

**What to do with the assets instead.** The Graphene primitives are not wasted, they are mis-aimed. The complaints that actually recur are about *review throughput* and *agents clobbering each other*, not about audit evidence: "turning senior engineers into full-time PR reviewers", "the bottleneck is review throughput, not review precision", "What task did this `spawn_agent` call give the child agent?". The fenced workspace, the isolated candidate ref, and the independent workspace audit are answers to *those* problems — a developer-felt, daily, seat-priced problem in a market where CodeRabbit charges $24-48/dev/mo — rather than to a compliance problem nobody has yet been asked about. That is a different dossier, and a better one.

**If Alex wants to test this anyway, the cost is three phone calls, not five weeks.** Ask three SOC 2 auditors whether they have ever asked a client about AI-authored code. If two say yes, reopen this file. Separately, contributing to [ossf/tac#628](https://github.com/ossf/tac/issues/628) is worth doing on career-capital grounds alone: it is free, public, open to anyone, and puts a junior in a working thread with the OpenSSF TAC. That is a real return from this research even though the product is a no.
