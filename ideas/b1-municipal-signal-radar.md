# Municipal Signal Radar for the Built-Environment Trades

**Slug:** b1-municipal-signal-radar  |  **Track:** B  |  **Researched:** 2026-08-30  |  **Status:** researched

## One-line pitch
A weekly Massachusetts-only feed of every new zoning application, special permit, site-plan review, subdivision and planning-board agenda item — parcel, applicant, project type, stage, and the engineer/attorney of record — sold to the small AEC and land-development firms that today find out about these projects only when the RFP drops, which is too late to win.

## Specific buyer

**Primary (sharpest):** Principal or business-development lead at a 5–50 person Massachusetts **civil engineering / land surveying / wetlands-and-environmental consulting firm**, or a **site-work contractor**. These firms get hired *by the applicant*, usually within 30–90 days of a filing appearing on a planning-board agenda. A filing is a literal, dated lead.

**Secondary:** preconstruction/BD at a 20–200 person general contractor; land-acquisition managers at regional homebuilders; land-use attorneys; commercial brokers doing site selection.

**Explicitly weak (evidence-based):** residential solar installers. I searched specifically for solar-installer complaints about municipal signal and found none — residential solar demand is homeowner-driven, not agenda-driven. Utility-scale solar developers do care (moratoria appear with little warning), but there are few of them and they are enterprise buyers.

**Where they are online:**
- ⚠️ VERIFIER: not_found - NAIOP Massachusetts — "close to 1,800 members," largest NAIOP chapter internationally (https://www.naiopma.org/who-is-naiop-ma/)
- AGC Massachusetts — public member directory at https://members.agcmass.org/members
- ULI Boston/New England, REFA Boston, CREW Boston, CoreNet New England, Greater Boston Real Estate Board, Builders of Color Coalition (all surfaced as active Boston CRE orgs)
- Boston Society of Architects (BSA Space, Fort Point); NESEA / BuildingEnergy Boston; Home Builders & Remodelers Association of MA
- **Notable negative:** no subreddit, Discord, Slack, or forum was found where these buyers discuss this pain. They are not an online-community-native audience. (Caveat: reddit.com returned HTTP 403 to every access method I have, and ContractorTalk sits behind a Tollbit bot paywall returning HTTP 402 — so the two largest likely veins could not be searched at all. Absence here is unproven, not disproven.)

**Where they are offline (Boston):**
- Every MA town planning board and ZBA meeting is public, remote-accessible, and posted with 48 hours' notice by statute. The applicant's engineer and attorney introduce themselves by name at the podium. This is a free, legal, high-signal buyer-identification channel.
- BPDA Article 80 public meetings in Boston.
- NAIOP MA events (forecasts, development tours, panels, golf tournament) — https://www.naiopma.org/events/
- Northeastern's co-op employer network reaches the Boston GC bench directly (Suffolk, Consigli, Shawmut, Skanska, Turner, BOND).

Sources 1–3 are vendor-published (FiscalNote/Curate marketing) — attributed and real, but marketing. Sources 4–9 are independent consumer complaints about the *paid substitutes*. ## Reachability (50 qualified buyers in 30 days, $0)

1. **Planning board and ZBA meetings themselves (highest signal, $0, unique to being local).** Every MA public body must post notice 48 hours ahead with "a listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed" (M.G.L. c. 30A § 20(b)-(c), https://www.mass.gov/files/documents/2016/08/xa/open-meeting-law-gl-c-30a-18-25.pdf). Most MA boards now stream. Sit in on 5 meetings across 5 towns; every applicant's civil engineer and land-use attorney states their firm on the record. That is a pre-qualified buyer list that no competitor outside Massachusetts can assemble, generated in ~8 hours of listening. 5 meetings/week × 4 weeks × ~3 named firms = ~60 named targets.
2. **AGC Massachusetts public member directory** — https://members.agcmass.org/members — named firms with public contact pages, filterable to Boston-area civil/site work.
3. **NAIOP Massachusetts, ~1,800 members**, https://www.naiopma.org/. Student/associate attendance at one event puts Alex physically in a room with several hundred of exactly this buyer. Check student rates.
4. **BSA Space (Fort Point) and BuildingEnergy Boston / NESEA** — free and low-cost public programming, walkable from campus.
5. **The applicant list itself is the outbound list.** Firms that appear on 3+ filings in a quarter are, by definition, the firms that live in this data — they are both the best prospects and the proof that the product works ("you filed in Natick, Framingham and Wellesley last month; here's who else did").
6. **Northeastern co-op network** — Suffolk, Consigli, Shawmut, BOND all take NEU co-ops. Warm intros to preconstruction leads are available through the university, not cold.

**Honest constraint:** there is no online watering hole. No Reddit thread, no Discord, no HN post. This buyer is reached by showing up, and Alex has 12 hrs/week. Reachability is *good but expensive in hours*, which is his scarce resource.

## Wedge

**The smallest thing one buyer pays for this month:** a Monday-morning email covering ~30–40 towns in MetroWest / Greater Boston, listing every new item that appeared on a planning board, ZBA, or conservation commission agenda in the past week that represents a *new private development filing* — with parcel address, applicant name, project type, stage, and the engineer or attorney of record, each row linking to the source PDF.

Sold to **one civil engineering or site-work firm at $149/month.** Not a platform, not a search UI, not alerts. One email, one persona, one region, hand-checked before it goes out for the first two months. Volume is small enough (a few hundred agenda documents per week) that a human-in-the-loop pass is affordable and makes the accuracy problem disappear at launch.

The reason this works as a first sale: the engineer-of-record field. It converts "a project exists" into "a competitor just won work I could have bid," which is a feeling, not a data point.

## Build estimate

**5–8 agent-days to a sellable MVP** (the sellable version is an email, not an app).

Components:
- **Ingest.** `civic-scraper` (https://github.com/biglocalnews/civic-scraper) is open source, actively maintained (last commit 2026-06-18, 73 stars, not archived) and supports "Civic Clerk, Civic Plus, Granicus, Legistar and PrimeGov" — confirmed to cover MA towns (Watertown, Springfield, Medford all run CivicClerk portals). Assume it covers 50–70% of target towns out of the box; the long tail is plain PDFs on bespoke town CMSs and is the real cost. **This single dependency is the largest cost reduction available.**
- **Boston specifically** is already structured: Article 80 Development Projects on Analyze Boston with an ArcGIS GeoServices REST API, https://data.boston.gov/dataset/article80-development-projects. Free, no scraping.
- **Extraction.** PDF → text (pdfplumber / OCR fallback for scans, which are common in small towns), then a small-model classification + field-extraction pass (parcel, applicant, type, stage, size, engineer/attorney). Every field carries a link back to the source page.
- **Storage.** Postgres. Dedupe on (town, board, meeting date, item text hash) — items recur across continued hearings, and a feed that re-alerts on a continuance is worse than no feed.
- **Delivery.** Templated weekly email via a free-tier transactional provider. Stripe payment link. No app, no login, until customer #5 asks for search.
- **Robots/ToS check per portal platform before writing a single crawler** — see Risks.

**Reusable assets: X-Scraper jobs.py (durable queue with leases/retries), read_service.compare_snapshots (re-observation dedupe across continued hearings), RegLineage validation/citations.py (source-quote integrity), mcp_server read-only gateway (agent access to the archive). civic-scraper (OSS) for CivicClerk/CivicPlus/Granicus.**

## Unit economics

- **Price:** $149/month (wedge), $99 entry / $299 multi-seat later.
- **Model/API cost:** the corpus is shared across all customers — this is a crawl cost, not a per-user cost. Estimate ~2,000–3,000 agenda/packet documents per month across ~250 MA towns × ~4 boards; after PDF text extraction, roughly 8k input / 800 output tokens per doc → on the order of 20M input + 2M output tokens per month. At small/fast-model rates this lands in the **single-digit to low-tens of dollars per month, total, across all customers**. *Do not treat that dollar figure as final — verify against current published API pricing before committing, since I did not fetch a rate card.* Marginal model cost per additional customer ≈ **$0**.
- **Hosting:** $5–10/mo (single small VPS + managed Postgres free tier) — one cron, one queue, no scale problem at this volume.
- **Email:** free tier (well under 3,000 sends/mo).
- **Total recurring burn: ~$15–35/month**, inside the $40/month cap, and flat as customers are added.
- **Gross margin at 3 customers ($447/mo): ~93%.** At 20 customers: ~98%.

The economics are excellent because the cost is a fixed crawl and the revenue is per-seat. This is the strongest single attribute of the idea.

## Risks

- **Legal — low.** The underlying data is public by statute: MA Open Meeting Law requires notice "at least 48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays," containing "a listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed" (M.G.L. c. 30A § 20). No login, no paywall, no personal data beyond business-relevant public facts (applicant, engineer, attorney acting in a business capacity). Applicants are frequently individual homeowners, though — **surface the parcel and the project, not a private person's name, unless they are appearing as a business.**
- **Platform/ToS dependency — medium, and the main pre-build task.** The data lives behind five vendor portals (CivicClerk, CivicPlus, Granicus/Legistar, PrimeGov) plus hundreds of bespoke town sites. Each vendor's ToS and robots.txt must be checked individually before crawling; some may prohibit automated access even for public records. I hit two live examples of bot-monetization walls during this research (ContractorTalk returning HTTP 402 via Tollbit; G2 and TrustRadius returning 403) — the same pattern is spreading. A vendor turning on bot blocking breaks coverage for dozens of towns at once. Mitigation: prefer official APIs (Boston), prefer the town's own posted PDFs over the portal where both exist, and keep a per-town adapter registry so one platform change is not a total outage.
- **Incumbent response — high.** Three companies already ship this. **InfoScout.ai** does the identical product across "3,100+ U.S. counties" for the largest US homebuilders. **Curate/FiscalNote** scans "400,000+ meeting minutes & agendas" weekly across "12,000+ municipalities" — Massachusetts is almost certainly already in that corpus. **CivicIQ** markets itself as "Win Public Sector Deals Before the RFP Drops," 50,000+ jurisdictions, "6–18 months before RFPs are published." None of them is *targeting MA small trades*, but adding a state is a config change for them and a month of work for Alex. The defensible position is persona and locality, never data volume.
- **Free-substitute risk — realized, not hypothetical.** MA already publishes the Central Register weekly (statutorily mandated, free, all state/county/municipal design and construction bids) and runs COMMBUYS free for vendors. **Any pitch built on "find RFPs" is dead on arrival in Massachusetts.** The product must live entirely upstream of the RFP, in private-development permitting.
- **Accuracy liability — medium.** An LLM misreading a parcel, a unit count, or a dollar figure could cause a customer to misallocate a bid. Mitigation: every extracted field links to the source PDF page; the email frames itself as an index into public documents, not as verified fact; no dollar-size extraction in v1.
- **Distribution risk — the real one.** This buyer does not read Reddit, HN, or Product Hunt. There is no product-led growth path. Growth is Alex physically attending things, at 12 hours a week. That, not the crawler, is what will decide this.

## Kill criteria

- **By 2026-10-15:** 50 named MA firms contacted (from planning board attendance + AGC directory). **Fewer than 8 replies expressing concrete interest → kill.** Fewer than 3 replies → kill immediately, do not build the crawler.
- **By 2026-11-15:** **3 paying customers at ≥$99/month.** Fewer than 3 → kill or hand off.
- **By 2027-01-15:** **8 paying customers, ≥$1,000 MRR.** Below that, the hours-per-dollar is worse than any freelance alternative and the idea should be shelved.
- **Hard sequencing rule:** do not write the crawler before the 2026-10-15 gate. Sell the manually-assembled email first. The data is public and a human can compile 30 towns' worth in a few hours — that is the whole point of the wedge.

## Verdict

The thesis is right and the evidence for it is unusually clean: the value in municipal data is entirely upstream of the RFP, incumbents say so in their own marketing ("Most other tools only flag opportunities when the RFP is already out. That's too late for us."), the manual substitute costs two to four people, and the largest homebuilders in the country already pay InfoScout for precisely this signal. The unit economics are close to ideal for Alex's constraints — a fixed crawl cost of $15–35/month serving unlimited seats at $149, comfortably inside his $40 burn cap, with civic-scraper and Boston's open Article 80 API removing most of the build.

But three things cut against it hard, and none of them is technical. First, **I could not find a single person in these trades complaining about this in public.** Nine verbatim quotes are in this dossier and not one is an organic post from a small MA contractor saying they missed something. Reddit and ContractorTalk were both inaccessible to me, so this is unproven rather than disproven — but a researcher who cannot find the complaint should assume the buyer is not, in fact, walking around in pain. Second, **the RFP-discovery half of the brief is dead in Massachusetts**: the Central Register is free, weekly, statutorily mandated and statewide, and COMMBUYS is free for vendors. Only pre-application private-development signal has residual value, which narrows the product considerably. Third, **the competitive field is more crowded than the brief assumed** — not just Curate, but InfoScout (identical product, national, top-tier customers), CivicIQ (identical positioning), Citizen Portal AI (all 50 states), and Perci (already doing Boston).

The one genuine, non-copyable edge is that Alex is in Boston, Massachusetts is 351 town-level jurisdictions rather than counties (which is exactly why a county-oriented national crawler like InfoScout under-serves New England), and he can sit in the meetings where the buyers announce themselves. That edge is a *sales* edge, not a data edge. So the correct move is to invert the usual order: spend the next six weeks selling a hand-compiled email to firms he identified by listening to planning board Zooms, and write zero crawler code until three people have paid. If eight firms won't reply by 2026-10-15, the silence in the forums was telling the truth and this should die cheaply.

Proceed — as a sales experiment, not a build.

### Is there a gap?
There is a gap, but it is NOT the feed — and the feed is what the dossier currently proposes to build.

What I proved is cheap: 8/15 towns (53%) work with an off-the-shelf OSS scraper and zero lines of code; 4 more (27%) need 1-4 hours each of bespoke adapter; 3 (20%) are hard-blocked; 0 are PDF-only or scanned. Every PDF opened had a born-digital text layer. MassGIS supplies 2.56M parcels with owner names and assessed values for $0. Total reachable coverage is 12/15 = 80% in roughly 8-10 hours — under one week of Alex's 12 hrs/wk — at essentially $0/mo. The absence of technical risk IS the finding: there is no data moat, no scraping-expertise moat, and no cost moat. Any competent developer with an AI assistant reproduces this in a weekend.

Worse, the incumbent already ships the naive product for free. CivicPlus "Notify Me" offers per-board agenda email subscriptions for exactly Planning Board / ZBA / Conservation Commission in all 8 CivicPlus towns (verified live on Lexington, Natick, Concord, Braintree). A prospect who wants Lexington Planning Board agendas gets them free from the town in thirty seconds. Time for an incumbent to close a pure-feed product: zero. It is already closed.

Two structural constraints cap everyone equally. M.G.L. c.30A s.20(b) requires posting only 48 hours before a meeting excluding weekends and holidays, so no agenda-derived product can deliver a lead more than ~4 calendar days out — speed cannot be a differentiator because nobody can be fast. And there is no statewide aggregator to displace: "MassNotify" was the COVID exposure app, mass.gov's public meeting notices cover state bodies only, and zero of the 15 towns run Legistar.

The genuine gap is one layer up, in three parts. (1) Cross-town normalization: nobody wants ~45 Notify Me subscriptions across 15 towns, 3 boards and 6 platforms, and board taxonomy is genuinely inconsistent — Wellesley and Concord have no Conservation Commission in AgendaCenter at all, Waltham lists "Planning Department" and "Board of Survey and Planning" instead of a Planning Board, so a naive committee_name filter silently drops real meetings. (2) Entity extraction, which is the only defensible layer: the 47-page Marlborough packet I extracted names the applicant's attorney, the project (Mosher Lane / Forest Trail Subdivision), the city engineer and the conservation officer. Turning that into {address, applicant, attorney, engineer, project_type, parcel_id, owner, assessed_value, zoning} joined to MassGIS is work nobody ships and where an LLM pipeline is actually differentiated. (3) The pre-agenda signals that beat the 48-hour floor by weeks — Wellesley's per-board "Applications Under Review" pages carrying case number plus address (DR-2026-32 - 17 Monadnock Road; HDC-26-05 - 58 Cottage Street), Norwood's address-bearing PDF filenames ("1.26.24 119 Sixth St. - RDA Legal Notice - stamped.pdf"), and Waltham's monthly issued-permit reports. Fifteen fragile hand-maintained scrapers is exactly the unglamorous, breakage-prone work that creates a small moat precisely because it is annoying — call it 6-12 months for an incumbent to bother, and they mostly will not bother because it does not fit a CMS product line.

The commercially serious problem: Newton is blocked and Newton is the prize. It is the largest and wealthiest town in the set (30,253 parcels) and sits behind Granicus govAccess fronted by Akamai Bot Manager, returning 403 to curl, to WebFetch, and to a Googlebot user-agent — robots.txt itself is unreachable. Dedham is blocked identically. Hopkinton's agenda host says "User-agent: * / Disallow: /" unambiguously. Every plausible customer will ask for Newton first, and the only workaround is browser-fingerprint spoofing against a .gov domain for a commercial product. That is a deliberate exposure decision, not a technical detail, and it should be made explicitly rather than discovered later.

### What this changes in the dossier
- Replace the 'can we get the data' framing entirely. That question is answered: 80% coverage, 8-10 hours, ~$0/mo, zero OCR. The dossier's risk section should stop listing data acquisition as a risk and start listing 'nobody pays because the town emails it free' as the top risk.
- Add the CivicPlus Notify Me finding as the headline competitive fact, with the four verified URLs. All 8 CivicPlus towns already offer free per-board email subscriptions for Planning Board, ZBA and Conservation Commission. Any pitch containing the phrase 'we email you the agendas' must be cut — that is a checkbox the incumbent CMS ships at $0 in 53% of the target market.
- Rewrite the build plan to start with 3 towns, not 15. Lexington + Natick + Marlborough are one platform, zero custom adapters, ~2 hours to a working feed. Extract applicant/attorney/engineer/address from packets, join to MassGIS, put it in front of 10 land-use attorneys or site-work contractors. If they will not pay for 3 towns they will not pay for 15, and the other 12 adapters are wasted weeks. Do not spend 4 weeks reaching 100% coverage — that is the comfortable work and it is the wrong work.
- Move entity extraction from an implied feature to THE product thesis. The Marlborough packet evidence (47 pages, 15.8MB, born-digital, naming the applicant's attorney, the Forest Trail Subdivision project, the city engineer and the conservation officer (all four by name in the source PDF)) is the single strongest artifact in this dive and belongs in the dossier verbatim as proof the signal exists in extractable form.
