# LLM Model-Deprecation and API-Change Migration Audits

**Slug:** r2-llm-deprecation-migration-audit  |  **Track:** A  |  **Researched:** 2026-08-30  |  **Status:** researched  |  **Origin:** round 2 (asset-suggested)

## One-line pitch
A dated, one-time "your code dies on October 23" audit: scan a repo for every hardcoded retired model id, deprecated request parameter and sunset endpoint, report days-to-shutdown per file and line, and hand back the upgrade PR with a regression harness — sold as a fixed-price engagement, not a subscription.

## Specific buyer
**Title:** The one engineer who owns the LLM feature at a 2-20 person product company — founding engineer, staff backend, or head of AI. Secondary: agencies and dev shops maintaining 5-40 client apps that each call an LLM, and Shopify/marketplace app vendors facing platform version deadlines.

## Willingness-to-pay evidence (>= 3)

1. **The exact business model already exists, at 10x the brief's price.** Lapointe Labs — the author of the `lifeline` deprecation scanner (repo created 2026-08-04) — sells, verbatim from https://lapointelabs.com fetched 2026-08-30:
   > "AI implementation audit / $7,500 / 2 weeks"
   > "A written report of 15 to 25 pages, plus the work behind it."
   > "Half when access is granted at the start of week one, half on delivery"
   > "If the report doesn't identify at least three specific, reproducible defects, you don't pay."
   The scanner is free and the audit is $7,500. **This is simultaneously the strongest WTP datapoint in the dossier and the strongest competitive threat: someone ran exactly this play 26 days ago.** Note also what they charge for — retrieval paths, ranking, evals, agent/tool boundaries, context assembly — *not* deprecation. Deprecation is the free doorway; the eval work is the invoice.

2. **Code scanning has an established per-seat price.** Semgrep, https://semgrep.dev/pricing (fetched 2026-08-30): Free "for up to 10 contributors"; Teams **"Starting at $30 / month per contributor"** (Code $30, Supply Chain $30, Secrets $15); Enterprise custom. A 6-dev team pays $180/month for a static scanner. This is the ceiling a *scanner* can charge — and Semgrep's free tier covers exactly the 2-20-dev buyer described above.

3. **LLM-ops budgets exist and are healthy, but not for this.** Braintrust, https://www.braintrust.dev/pricing (2026-08-30): Starter $0, **Pro $249/month**, Enterprise custom. Portkey, https://portkey.ai/pricing (2026-08-30): Open Source free, Developer "Free Forever", **Production $49/month** plus "$9/Month for Every 100K Up to 3M Requests", Enterprise custom. **Neither product's feature list mentions retired-model-id detection.** Teams calling LLMs already pay $49-$249/month to somebody; they are not paying it for deprecation.

⚠️ VERIFIER: altered - 4. **The audit-first services pattern is durable in an analogous market.** FastRuby.io (https://www.fastruby.io/, 2026-08-30) has upgraded "over 100 applications" since 2012 and begins "most projects with an audit to create 'The Roadmap' — a detailed report of required work." No price is published, which is itself the tell: framework-upgrade shops quote per engagement rather than list. This validates *audit → migration engagement* as a business shape, and validates that it is a **services business**, not a SaaS.

5. **Manual-cost arithmetic (carried over from `b5-api-change-watchdog.md`, computed there from BLS May 2025 median $135,980 for software developers).** Fully loaded ~$95/hr. A model migration that requires re-tuning rather than a string swap is a 1-3 day engineering task = **$760-$2,280** of internal cost. That comfortably supports a $500-$1,500 fixed price *if* the buyer believes the re-tuning risk. It does not support anything for a pure string swap, which — per evidence item 5 — is "trivial."

**Price anchors summary:** free scanners at $0 (four of them, below), Semgrep at $30/dev/mo, Portkey at $49/mo, Braintrust at $249/mo, Lapointe Labs at $7,500/engagement. The gap between $0 and $7,500 is where this idea lives, and there is no observed transaction anywhere in that gap for deprecation work specifically.

## Reachability (50 qualified buyers in 30 days, $0)

Honest assessment: **this is the weakest leg of the idea, and it is weak in a specific, diagnosable way.**

Workable channels:
1. **GitHub code search is a perfect qualification list and a terrible contact list.** `gh api search/code -f q='"gpt-4-0613"'` returns 1,976 public files today, `"gpt-3.5-turbo"` 19,520. Free, public, in-ToS to read. But the overwhelming majority are tutorials, forks, dead side projects and vendored samples, not companies with budget — and turning a repo into a *permitted* contact requires a maintainer who has invited contact (README email, "we're hiring", public Discord). Realistic yield after that filter: single-digit percent. This is identification, not permission; bulk-mailing repo owners is spam and is out.
2. **OpenAI Developer Community, ethically.** The threads exist and are public, but the traffic is 38-144 views for developer deprecation threads. Answering honestly with a free tool is legitimate; the audience is too small to produce 50 buyers.
3. **The Assistants API sunset (2026-08-26, 7,027 views) was the one real gathering** — and it has already happened. The equivalent gathering for the 2026-10-23 wave has not formed on any channel I could reach.
4. **A free MIT scanner as the doorway.** This is the only credible PLG path, and it is exactly what four other people already did in the last six weeks with 0 GitHub stars between them and ~1,660 total npm downloads. Expected organic yield: near zero.
5. **Boston, in person.** Startup Boston Week is in September, BNT runs monthly, CIC/Kendall is 20 minutes away, and Northeastern's co-op network is a genuine unfair advantage. Alex can hold 30-45 qualified conversations across three events. **But the qualifying question here is bad for a room**: "which model id is hardcoded in your repo?" is not something a founder knows standing up with a drink, and the honest answer to "what happens on October 23" is usually "I'll change a string." Compare with the B6 clerk ideas, where the qualifying question is one the buyer can answer instantly.
6. **Agencies are the better shape.** One agency maintaining 20 client apps is 20 repos and one invoice. Boston has a dense dev-shop population. This is the channel worth testing, and it is a small number of high-value conversations, not 50.

**Verdict on reachability: 50 *qualified* buyers in 30 days at $0 is not achievable. 5-10 agency conversations is.**

## Build estimate
**Agent-days to a sellable MVP: 2-3.** (Down from the 6-9 estimated in B5, because the data half is already free.)

- Deprecation registry: **0.5 days**, not 1.5. LiteLLM's `model_prices_and_context_window.json` is MIT-licensed and already carries `deprecation_date` on **611 of 3,408 entries** (verified 2026-08-30 by fetching the raw file). ChirpPal publishes a free hand-verified OpenAI/Anthropic/Google retirement list. Work reduces to reconciling those against the four vendor pages and adding the ~60 OpenAI ids above.
- Call-site matcher: **1 day.** Model ids are string literals; ripgrep over source + config + env-var defaults + notebooks gets ~95% of them. Tree-sitter is overkill for strings. The genuinely hard cases (ids assembled from variables, ids in a database, ids in a vendor dashboard prompt) are not solvable by static scanning at all and should be named as out-of-scope rather than half-built.
- Parameter/endpoint rules: **0.5 days.** `temperature`/`top_p`/`top_k` on Claude 4.7+, Assistants API → Responses API, `v1/prompts`, Evals API, `client.query()` on Shopify. This is the differentiated half.
- Regression harness generator (golden set from prod logs or from the repo's own tests, run old model vs new model, diff and score): **1 day.** **This is the part worth building; it is the part that is actually sold.**
- Report + `gh pr create`: **0.25 days.**

Deliberately skipped: a hosted SaaS, a dashboard, multi-tenant auth, a subscription. Every one of those is contradicted by the pricing evidence.

**Reusable assets: Nemisis differential runner (base vs migrated candidate on the same prompt set); Graphene workspace_audit for touched-file truth; Graft for call-site graph.**

## Unit economics
- **Price:** $1,500 per repo engagement. Target 2-4 in the 54 days before 2026-10-23, then reassess.
- **Delivery cost:** ~6-10 hours of Alex's time per engagement (agent does the scan and PR draft; Alex reviews, runs the harness, writes the memo). At a 12 hr/week budget that is **one engagement per week, maximum two in flight**.
- **LLM cost per engagement:** repo scan is regex, ~$0. PR drafting ~30k in / 8k out on a Sonnet-class model ≈ $0.21. Regression harness: 100 cases x 2 models x ~4k tokens ≈ $2-6 depending on the models under test (the client's own key should pay for their model calls — insist on this). **Under $10 per engagement.**
- **Fixed burn:** domain ~$1.20/month amortized; GitHub Actions free tier; a static landing page on Cloudflare Pages/GitHub Pages **$0**; Stripe at 2.9%+30c only on revenue. **Total recurring burn: ~$2/month, far under the $40 ceiling.** No server is needed — the CLI runs on the client's machine, which is also the correct answer to the "I'm not uploading my source" objection (and is exactly how `lifeline` positions itself: "No account, API key, source upload, or configuration is required").
- **Gross margin:** ~99% on token cost; the real cost is Alex's 12 hrs/week, which is the binding constraint. **This is a services business with services economics, not a SaaS.** Two engagements = $3,000 = the entire capital base tripled, but it does not recur.

## Risks
- **Four free direct competitors shipped in the last six weeks.** `lapointelabs/lifeline` (2026-08-04), `daryl-micah/modeldrift-zerops` (2026-08-08), `thossullivan/model-eol` (2026-07-25 / npm 2026-08-03), `chirppal-mcp` + ChirpPal Model Deprecation Radar (2026-07-20). All free. `lifeline` already emits file:line, shutdown date, replacement, SARIF for GitHub code scanning, and "a migration handoff." **Whatever Alex builds as the free doorway is a fourth entrant into a room where nobody clapped for the first three.**
- **The data moat does not exist.** 611 `deprecation_date` entries in LiteLLM (MIT), plus ChirpPal's free hand-verified list, plus four vendor pages that are already machine-readable enough for an LLM to parse in one pass.
- **The failure mode is cheap to fix.** Per `tacoooooooo` (2026-07-21): "dropping in a new model is trivial." A 404 `model_not_found` is unambiguous, fires immediately, and is fixed by editing a string. That is the opposite of the expensive, ambiguous, slow-burning pain that justifies a purchase order. The *expensive* version (silent quality regression, the PartsplanAI 16-hour incident in B5, `neosmalt`'s "no error, just missing data downstream") is real but is not what a scanner detects.
- **Vendors are closing the gap themselves.** Anthropic ships a Console usage export explicitly "To help identify usage of deprecated models" with per-key, per-model breakdown. Azure exposes `lifecycleStatus`, `deprecation.inference` and per-SKU `deprecationDate` through a public Models API and **auto-upgrades** Standard deployments. AWS exposes `modelLifecycle` on `GetFoundationModel`. Three of the five platforms already answer "am I affected" from the *runtime* side, which is more accurate than static scanning.
- **The deadline is a one-shot.** 2026-10-23 is 54 days out. Miss it and the next comparable wave is 2026-12-11, then 2027-01-20. A dated-deadline business has no baseline demand between deadlines.
- **Positioning risk from the free tool.** If Alex publishes a scanner and someone finds `lifeline` or `model-eol` first, the differentiation collapses to "and I also do the migration" — which is consulting, sold on trust and relationships, not on the tool.
- **Not a recurring-revenue business.** Every piece of pricing evidence points to services (Lapointe $7,500/engagement, FastRuby per-engagement) or to $0. This satisfies Alex's money priority #2, not #1.

## Kill criteria
- **By 2026-09-15 (16 days):** if 10 agency/small-team conversations produce fewer than 2 people who say "yes, quote me for that," stop. The 2026-10-23 deadline is the single best sales moment this idea will ever have; failing to convert against a 54-day hard deadline means it never converts.
- **By 2026-10-23:** if zero engagements have been *paid for* (not agreed to — paid), kill outright.
- **Immediately, if the scanner is built first.** Building before selling here is the trap: three people already did it and have 0 stars, 0 HN mentions, and ~1,660 lifetime npm downloads between them. Sell the engagement by hand, deliver the first one with ripgrep and a text editor, and only then decide whether the tool is worth writing.
- **If two consecutive prospects say "we just change the string,"** the eval-harness framing has failed and the whole idea is dead — because the string swap is genuinely free.

## Verdict
**Weak pursue — as a hand-sold service against the 2026-10-23 deadline, and only if it can be sold before it is built. Do not build the scanner. Do not build the SaaS.**

The dated evidence here is the best in the portfolio: I can name the exact day (2026-10-23, 54 days out), the exact model ids (`gpt-3.5-turbo`, `gpt-4-0613`, `gpt-4-turbo`, `o1`, `o3-mini`, `o4-mini`, `gpt-4.1-nano`, `gpt-4o-2024-05-13`, plus five fine-tuned families), the exact error text developers get (`code: 'model_not_found'`, and a `400 '\`temperature\` is deprecated for this model'` for the parameter case), and the exact scale of exposure (19,520 public files mentioning `gpt-3.5-turbo`; 427M monthly PyPI installs of `openai`). That is far more concrete than the general API-changelog idea that round 1 killed.

And it does not matter as much as it should, for three reasons the research is unambiguous about.

**First, four people built this free tool in the last six weeks and nobody noticed.** `lifeline` (2026-08-04), `modeldrift-zerops` (2026-08-08), `model-eol` (2026-07-25), ChirpPal Radar (2026-07-20). Combined GitHub stars: **zero**. Combined lifetime npm downloads: about 1,660. Zero Hacker News mentions. `lifeline` already ships file:line output, shutdown dates, replacements, SARIF and a Markdown migration handoff — the entire product described in the brief — and has 0 stars. Shipping a fifth free scanner is not a wedge; it is joining a queue.

**Second, the one competitor with a business model behind it points at the real answer, and the real answer is not deprecation.** Lapointe Labs gives the scanner away and charges **$7,500 for a two-week AI implementation audit** covering retrieval, ranking, evals, agent boundaries and context assembly. They are not selling the migration. They are using "your code dies October 23" as a door-opener for the eval work — because the eval work is what is expensive, and because, as `tacoooooooo` put it on 2026-07-21, "dropping in a new model is trivial." The scan finds a string. The invoice has to be for something harder than finding a string.

**Third, the buyer cannot be reached at the required volume.** GitHub code search identifies 19,520 candidate files and permits almost no contact. Reddit is 403. The largest developer-side deprecation threads found anywhere have 38 to 144 views. Alex's genuine Boston advantage — CIC, Startup Boston Week, the NEU co-op network — does not help here the way it helps the B6 clerk ideas, because "which model id is hardcoded in your codebase" is not a question a founder can answer standing up, and the honest answer is usually a shrug.

**What I would actually do with the next 16 days, at zero build cost.** Take the verified deadline table above. Identify 10-15 Boston-area agencies and 5-20-person product teams with public LLM features. Offer, by hand, in a real conversation: *"$1,500, five days, one repo — I inventory every retiring model id and deprecated parameter with days-to-shutdown, open the upgrade PR, and give you a 50-case regression harness that scores old model vs new so you know your outputs did not get worse."* If two people say yes before 2026-09-15, deliver those two with ripgrep and a text editor and decide afterward whether any code deserves to be written. If fewer than two say yes against the single most favorable deadline this idea will ever have, this is a clean, well-documented no — and the evidence collected here (the deadline calendar, the code-search exposure counts, the four dead competitors) transfers directly to the adjacent ideas below, which have better shapes.

The pain is real and dated. The purchase is not evidenced. That combination is exactly what round 1 found for B5, and finding it again in the highest-velocity slice of the same market is itself the strongest signal in this dossier.
