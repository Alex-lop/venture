# Massachusetts Rental Compliance Packet Generator

**Slug:** r2-ma-rental-compliance-packet  |  **Track:** B  |  **Researched:** 2026-08-30  |  **Status:** researched  |  **Origin:** round 2 (asset-suggested)

## One-line pitch
A $29–49/month tool that assembles the correct Massachusetts lease-signing compliance packet per unit — the new DPH Tenant Lead Law Notification, the c.186 §15B security-deposit receipt and statement of condition, the Notice of Occupants' Legal Rights, smoke/CO and Boston registration reminders — so a small landlord stops hand-assembling it every time a unit turns.

**Research finding that changes the pitch:** the packet already exists, twice, for free. MassLandlords ships a versioned forms library plus an "Agreement Checklist (Latest Version: 11)" to its 2,611 dues-paying members, and published the two-step workaround for the locked lead-law PDF in the same article that announced the problem. TurboTenant published a complete, correct enumeration of every Massachusetts required disclosure — naming the lead-law certification form specifically — as free SEO content on 2026-07-27. Avail gives away state-specific leases with "locally-generated clauses, rules, and disclosures" at $0/unit. The regulatory *event* is real and freshly dated; the recurring *pain* it was supposed to create is not evidenced anywhere I could reach.

## Specific buyer
Massachusetts landlords and small property managers holding **2–50 units of pre-1978 housing stock**, concentrated in Boston/Worcester/Springfield, who sign a handful of new leases a year and self-manage.

Sharpest concentration: **MassLandlords, Inc.** — "Join 2,611 Members as of August 3 and Counting" (masslandlords.net/join, fetched 2026-08-30). Dues are "calculated on the basis of units owned or managed and location" (masslandlords.net/dues). This is simultaneously the buyer list, the distribution channel, and the incumbent, which is the central structural problem with this idea.

Secondary: Boston-registered rental owners. Boston requires every rental to register annually ($25/unit first-time, $15/unit renewal, capped at $2,500/building), deadline July 1, with a **$300/month penalty** for non-registration (boston.gov/rental-registration, last updated 10/30/25). That list is a public register of exactly this buyer.

## Unit economics
At $39/mo, 20 customers = **$780 MRR / $9,360 ARR**.

- Stripe: 2.9% + $0.30 → ~$1.43/customer/mo → $28.60/mo at 20 customers.
- Hosting: Fly.io/Render small instance + Postgres, ~$10–15/mo. Domain ~$12/yr.
- **Fixed burn ≈ $25–30/mo — fits under the $40/month ceiling, but only barely, and only before the attorney.**
- Gross margin ~93% on paper.
- **Ignored in that math:** the brief says "with human review." Human review is Alex, at 12 hrs/week, in perpetuity, on documents carrying treble-damages exposure. At 20 customers × ~4 lease signings/yr × 20 min of review = ~27 hrs/year, tolerable. At 200 customers it is a part-time job with unlimited downside and no insurance.
- **E&O insurance is not in the budget and is not optional for this product.** Tech E&O for a document-generation product typically starts around $1,500–3,000/yr. That is 2–3× the total capital and would consume 20–30% of revenue at 20 customers.

**The economics only work if the product stops generating the legally operative document.** Which is the same as saying: build the calendar, not the packet.

## Risks

**1. The pain has a published free workaround, written by the channel.** MassLandlords told 2,611 members to "fill out the lead notification separately and save it as a new PDF" on 2026-05-26. That is the whole job. A $39/mo subscription competes with save-as.

**2. The channel is the incumbent.** MassLandlords owns the forms, versions them, licenses them ("leave the copyright notice and terms in the footer... please write 'Adapted under license from' prior to our copyright mark"), sells access to them through per-unit dues, sells a $101/hr helpline, and sells a $275 crash course. Selling a competing $39/mo packet generator *to their members, at their events* is both commercially hopeless and a bad-faith use of the only good distribution channel available.

**3. Liability is the worst available profile for this principal.** Massachusetts imposes **strict liability** for lead poisoning. c.186 §15B(7) awards **treble damages plus 5% interest plus attorney's fees** for deposit-handling failures. EPA disclosure violations have already cost one Boston landlord ~$84,000 "just for paperwork violations." A 20-year-old with $1,000, no E&O, and a disclaimer is not a credible counterparty for that risk.

**4. UPL boundary is real and unresolved.** MassLandlords, which employs experienced non-attorney staff, still writes "**We do not give legal advice. We give business advice and legal information**" on its helpline page. That is a workable line — sell the *assembly and the reminder*, never the *advice about which form applies to your situation*. But "the tool decided your unit needs form X" is on the wrong side of it. I could not fetch M.G.L. c.221 §46A or mass.gov's UPL page directly today (403), so this is characterized from MassLandlords' own posture, not from the statute. **Resolve before writing code.**

**5. Incumbents are already moving on the square.** TurboTenant published the definitive free MA disclosure guide five weeks ago and ships a free AI lease-compliance audit. Avail (Realtor.com) gives away "locally-generated clauses, rules, and disclosures." Neither needs to be excellent — they need to be free and adequate, and they are.

**6. Frequency is low.** A 2–10 unit landlord signs a handful of leases a year. A monthly subscription for a several-times-a-year job has a structural churn problem: the buyer notices they aren't using it. That TurboTenant Trustpilot review is exactly this failure mode, at $149/yr, already.

**7. Market ceiling is small and knowable.** MassLandlords' entire paying membership is 2,611. At $39/mo, 5% penetration = ~$61k ARR. That is the realistic top of the wedge, not the floor.

## Kill criteria
- **Already met, on the evidence:** if no Massachusetts landlord can be found in public saying the packet is a problem worth paying to remove, do not build a subscription against it. **Two MassLandlords articles on this exact topic, 0 comments each; zero HN hits; no forum thread found. This criterion has fired.**
- **By 2026-09-30:** attend one MassLandlords Boston office hours. If fewer than **8 of 20** landlords say the new lead form is a recurring annoyance *after* being shown the save-as workaround they may not know about, kill permanently.
- **Immediate kill:** if MassLandlords indicates they would not white-label or partner. Competing with them for their own members is not a viable plan for this principal.
- **Immediate kill:** if a Massachusetts attorney says a non-lawyer product that selects which statutory forms apply to a given tenancy crosses the UPL line, or if generating the c.186 §15B statement of condition without attorney review is uninsurable at this scale.
- **Immediate kill:** if there is no lawful, ToS-compliant way to determine pre-1978 status statewide from public data. Without it the product is a form-filler, and form-fillers cost $99/yr (ezLandlordForms) or $0 (Avail).

## Verdict
**Do not build this as a subscription. The regulatory event is real; the recurring pain it was supposed to create is not.**

The round-1 hypothesis was good and worth testing: a brand-new, ten-page, password-protected, mandatory-at-every-signing state form, issued without notice-and-comment, in a state with strict liability and treble damages, sold into a trade association with 2,611 members and a physical door in Boston. That is a legitimate shape for a wedge.

Three findings closed it. First, MassLandlords published the fix in the same article as the problem — "fill out the lead notification separately and save it as a new PDF" — on 2026-05-26, and that article has zero comments, as does their security-deposit article. Second, the packet already ships: MassLandlords maintains an "Agreement Checklist (Latest Version: 11)" plus every constituent form, versioned, inside dues that 2,611 people already pay, with the Lead Law Notification marked **Public**. Third, on 2026-07-27 TurboTenant published the complete and correct Massachusetts disclosure map — naming the Tenant Lead Law Notification and Certification form specifically — as free SEO content, while Avail puts "locally-generated clauses, rules, and disclosures powered by LocalAssist" in a **$0/unit** tier. Selling a $39/mo assembler into that is selling above TurboTenant Pro to do less.

Against that, the downside is the worst available to this principal: strict liability, treble damages plus attorney's fees under §15B(7), a documented $84,000 Boston paperwork fine, an unresolved UPL boundary that the trade association itself handles with a written disclaimer, an attorney review that costs more than the entire $1,000 of capital, and E&O insurance at 2–3× capital. A twenty-year-old should not be the party that publishes the document a Housing Court reads.

**What is worth keeping is the channel, not the product.** Reachability scored 4 — the highest number in this dossier — and it is portable. Alex can be in a room with twenty Massachusetts landlords for a T fare, and that is a genuine, rare asset. The right use of it is one evening of discovery at a MassLandlords office hours before September 30, asking what actually costs them time, rather than forty hours building the thing this dossier says they already have for free. The two adjacent products below — a Boston/MA compliance *deadline* tracker, and a §15B deposit-interest calculator — came out of this research, carry no document-drafting liability, have hard money consequences attached ($300/month, treble the interest), and would use the same channel. If anything in this vertical gets built, it should be one of those.

**Recommendation: kill the packet generator. Keep the Massachusetts landlord channel. Spend one T fare, not forty hours.**
