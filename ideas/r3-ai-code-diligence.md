# AI-code-composition technical due diligence for PE/VC and M&A

**Slug:** r3-ai-code-diligence  |  **Track:** A/B hybrid  |  **Researched:** 2026-08-30  |  **Status:** researched  |  **Origin:** round 3 — spawned by the adversarial verification pass on [`r2-ai-code-provenance-receipts.md`](r2-ai-code-provenance-receipts.md), which named "the M&A / PE technical-due-diligence buyer" as that dossier's **biggest omission**

## One-line pitch
A fixed-price forensic report on a target company's git history that tells an investment committee how much of the codebase was written by AI agents, under what human review, with what test discrimination, and what license/copyright exposure that creates — the "GenAI Bill of Materials" line item that Sema promised the M&A market in 2024 and nobody has actually shipped into a diligence deliverable by 2026.

## Specific buyer

**Title:** the technical due-diligence lead — either (a) an operating/value-creation partner or in-house tech-DD person at a PE firm or growth investor, or (b) an engagement lead at the diligence shop the firm hires.

**Why this buyer was worth testing at all:** the r2 verification pass found that the one company charging real money to answer "how much of this code did an AI write" — Sema — sells to *investors*, not to CTOs. Sema's own homepage, fetched 2026-08-30: "We work with investors to gauge the health of software within each investment opportunity," and "In 2023, Sema served **7 of the 9 largest global investors**, as well as market-leading strategic investors, and private equity and venture capital firms" ([semasoftware.com](https://www.semasoftware.com/), fetched 2026-08-30). That is a named, moneyed, non-hypothetical segment, which is more than the SOC 2 auditor hypothesis ever produced.

**Boston relevance:** Boston is genuinely a PE hub (Bain Capital, TA Associates, Summit Partners, Advent, Charlesbank, Great Hill, Thoma Bravo's Boston office), and — the more useful fact — **Black Duck Software is headquartered at 800 District Ave. Ste 201, Burlington, MA 01803** (footer of every blackduck.com page, fetched 2026-08-30), 20 minutes up 93 from Northeastern. The largest code-audit-for-M&A business in the world is a local employer, and it publishes an **audit hotline: +1 781.425.4444** ([blackduck.com/solutions/mergers-acquisitions.html](https://www.blackduck.com/solutions/mergers-acquisitions.html), fetched 2026-08-30).

Twenty-nine months later, Black Duck — the firm that has audited "thousands of M&A deals" — published its 2026 framework for exactly this and left AI *authorship* out of it entirely (see Pain evidence #3).
- **The company that owned this thesis has left it.** Sema's blog has published **nothing about GenAI code since October 2024**; its only 2026 posts are "Search your Slack conversations with AI" (April 7, 2026) and "Liz - Zoom Integration Documentation" (March 12, 2026) ([semasoftware.com/blog](https://www.semasoftware.com/blog), fetched 2026-08-30). So: the buyer has budget, the buyer buys code diligence every deal, and the buyer has not been observed asking for *this*.

**Where they read:** deal-shop client alerts (Black Duck, Crosslake, Silverthread), Axial/ACG events, LinkedIn deal-professional orbit, law-firm client alerts. Notably **not** Hacker News — see the graveyard.

## Reachability (50 qualified buyers in 30 days, $0)

**Not achievable. This is the hardest-to-reach buyer in the entire idea set**, harder than the SOC 2 compliance owner from r2, and the reasons are structural rather than about effort.

1. **Deal professionals do not take unsolicited meetings about tooling from students.** There is no OpenSSF-equivalent open working group for PE tech diligence. NEVCA — the obvious Boston body — states membership "is open to **venture capital firms**, pre-seed to growth equity" ([newenglandvc.org](https://www.newenglandvc.org/), fetched 2026-08-30): firms, not individuals, not students.
2. **Venture Café Cambridge is the one genuinely open door.** Free weekly Thursday gatherings at CIC Kendall ([venturecafecambridge.org](https://venturecafecambridge.org/), fetched 2026-08-30), open to anyone, and investors do attend. This is a real $0 channel and it is worth 2-4 conversations a month, not 50.
3. **The diligence shops do not hire students.** Crosslake's careers page ends: "We're always accepting inquiries and applications from **experienced technology practitioners**" ([crosslaketech.com/crosslake-careers](https://crosslaketech.com/crosslake-careers/), fetched 2026-08-30). Fully remote, senior-practitioner model. I found no intern, co-op, or contractor track. Crosslake's model is credibility-for-hire; a junior is the opposite of the product.
4. **Northeastern co-op is the strongest asset here and it is not a sales channel.** A co-op at Black Duck (Burlington, 20 min away) or at a Boston PE firm's value-creation team would put Alex inside the buying process. That is a 6-month employment path with a real return — but it is a *job*, not a venture, and it converts this dossier into career capital rather than revenue. I could not verify Northeastern's PE/VC co-op employer list read-only (NUworks is authenticated).
5. **Cold outbound to the diligence shops** is the only scalable motion, and the pitch would be "let me subcontract on your engagements" — which runs into the E&O/liability wall in Risks below.

**Honest number: 5-10 qualified conversations in 30 days at $0, with maybe one from a warm Northeastern alumni intro.** Not 50.

## Wedge

The brief offers two. They are not equally good.

**The $1,500 "AI authorship & review-hygiene scan" on one repo: no.** It requires a target company's source code under NDA before a deal closes. A first-time solo vendor with no entity, no E&O policy, and no track record will not be given data-room access to a target's repository — that access is the single most protected artifact in a tech deal, and it is granted to Black Duck precisely because Black Duck has done "thousands" of them and carries insurance. There is no version of this where the first sale happens before the credential exists.

**The free public benchmark: yes, and it is the only thing here worth doing.** Run the forensic pass over a corpus of *public* repos — no NDA, no client, no liability — and publish: what fraction of merged commits in N popular OSS projects carry agent trailers (`Co-Authored-By: Claude`, `Generated with Claude Code`, Codex/Cursor signatures), how that trended 2024→2026, what fraction of agent-trailed PRs had a human review with a non-trivial comment, and how test-diff discrimination differs between agent-trailed and human commits. Cost: a weekend of compute and Alex's existing primitives. It is publishable, citable, and it is the *only* artifact that could make a diligence shop call a student.

**Before either, the cheap falsifying test, which costs three conversations and zero code:** ask three tech-DD practitioners — one at Black Duck (the hotline is published), one at a Boston PE value-creation team, one independent — *"In your last ten diligences, did any client ask what share of the target's codebase was written by AI coding agents?"* If fewer than two say yes, this file is closed. The public evidence predicts zero yes, because the market leader's own February 2026 framework does not ask that question.

## Unit economics

- **Recurring burn:** ~$0. Runs locally against a clone. Comfortably under the $40/mo cap.
- **Price anchor:** CodeDD's €4,490 (~$4,900) full audit ceilings the wedge. A single-repo authorship scan realistically prices at **$1,000-2,500**.
- **Deal math if it worked:** 6 scans/year at $1,500 = **$9,000/yr, one-time revenue, zero recurring**. At 12 hrs/week that is a meaningful fraction of the year's capacity for a number that does not compound.
- **What would compound and cannot be had:** the benchmark corpus. Crosslake sells on "data from more than 6,000 prior technology M&A transactions"; Sema on "$1 trillion" of code evaluated. In this market the dataset *is* the product and it accrues from doing deals you cannot get access to. A newcomer is structurally locked out of the only compounding asset — unless the corpus is public repos, which is the wedge above and is worth exactly zero dollars directly.
- **CAC:** relationship-shaped and slow. Diligence work arrives by referral inside a small professional network with 20-year memories.

## Risks

1. **Liability on an opinion used in a deal — the decisive one.** A diligence report is relied upon by an investment committee pricing a transaction. If it says "12% agent-authored, low copyleft exposure" and the acquirer later finds otherwise, the exposure is the deal, not the fee. Black Duck can absorb this (insurance, entity, thousands of engagements, a **Legal Specialist Certification Course** for interpreting its analysis). A student with $1,000 of capital cannot buy E&O insurance at a price that makes a $1,500 report rational. **This alone is close to fatal for the paid version.**
2. **Access to target code under NDA.** Pre-close source access is the most tightly held artifact in a tech deal. The vendors who get it get it on institutional credibility. There is no bootstrapping path through this door that starts with a first customer.
3. **The signal is trivially erasable — and the agent vendor ships the eraser.** The r2 dossier documented Claude Code's "undercover mode" system prompt: *"NEVER include in commit messages or PR descriptions: The phrase 'Claude Code' or any mention that you are an AI - Co-Authored-By lines or any other attribution"* ([HN 47591989](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47591989), 2026-03-31). Git-trailer forensics measures **only the honest**. A seller motivated to look human strips trailers with one `git filter-repo`. A diligence opinion built on a defeatable signal is worse than no opinion, and any competent counsel will say so in the first meeting.
4. **The incumbents already answer the question that has money attached — differently and better.** The legally consequential question is not "did an agent type this" but "does this code carry someone else's licence." Black Duck answers that with snippet matching against the **KnowledgeBase™** ("petabytes of open source code and metadata") via the [Open Source Snippet API](https://www.blackduck.com/blog/analyze-ai-generated-code-black-duck-snippet-api.html) (Feb 3, 2025): "there are times when their output is similar enough to open source projects that it can be matched back to their source." That approach is immune to trailer-stripping because it reads the code, not the metadata. **Provenance forensics loses to content matching on the one question a lawyer cares about.**
5. **The copyright hook is real but narrower than it looks.** The U.S. Copyright Office is unambiguous that "applicants have a **duty to disclose the inclusion of AI-generated content** in a work submitted for registration and to provide a brief explanation of the human author's contributions" (88 FR 16190, effective 2023-03-16, [federalregister.gov](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2023/03/16/2023-05321/copyright-registration-guidance-works-containing-material-generated-by-artificial-intelligence)). **But that duty attaches only on registration**, and most SaaS companies never register source-code copyrights. And the Office's January 2025 Part 2 report concludes: "existing legal doctrines are **adequate and appropriate** to resolve questions of copyrightability... **the Office does not recommend a change in the law**" ([Copyrightability Report](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/Copyright-and-Artificial-Intelligence-Part-2-Copyrightability-Report.pdf), p. 40-41). **No forcing function, no deadline, no new disclosure regime for software.**
6. **F-1 status — unresolved and material.** Paid contract diligence work for third parties is not obviously covered by CPT/OPT, and a wrong guess here is a status problem, not a business problem. Must be settled with Northeastern OGS *before* invoicing anyone. Flagged, not researched (not resolvable read-only).
7. **The category's own champion left.** Sema published nothing on GenAI code after October 2024 and now sells a delivery agent (Liz) and an AI-insurance program (ARIA). Either the market did not materialize, or it did and Sema lost — both are bad.
8. **The HN graveyard repeats exactly.** Every launch in this category since 2025 scored 1-3 points and drew **zero comments**: CodeDD "automating software auditing for investment rounds" ([43026898](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43026898), 1 pt, 0 comments), CodeJourney "utility to create evidences for technical due diligence" ([49292076](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49292076), 1 pt, 0 comments), "Leverage AI in Technical Due Diligence Engagements" ([44499602](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44499602), 1 pt), model-due-diligence ([48515575](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48515575), 3 pts).

## Kill criteria

Pre-committed, checkable, cheap. KC1 is the whole decision.

1. **Practitioner check (do this first; costs three conversations).** Ask three technical-DD practitioners: *"In your last ten engagements, did a client ask what share of the target's codebase was written by AI coding agents?"* **Fewer than two yes → kill.** Public evidence predicts zero, because Black Duck's own February 2026 M&A AI framework does not contain the question.
2. **Erasability check.** Ask the same three: *"If a seller stripped agent trailers from git history before the data room opened, would my report be worthless?"* **Two or more yes → kill the paid product outright** (keep the free benchmark).
3. **Insurance check.** Get one written E&O quote for a solo consultant issuing code-diligence opinions relied on in transactions. **If the annual premium exceeds $3,000 — i.e. two full engagements — kill.** This is a phone call, not a project.
4. **Access check.** Within 60 days, has any party offered read access to a real target repo under NDA? **No → kill.** Without this there is no product, only a benchmark.
5. **Incumbent check.** If Black Duck, Crosslake, Silverthread, CAST, or CodeDD ships a named "AI authorship / GenAI BOM" line item in its diligence deliverable → **kill immediately**; they have the deal flow, the corpus and the insurance.
6. **Benchmark traction check.** If the free public benchmark, once published, produces **zero inbound from any diligence shop or investor in 60 days → kill the whole thread**, keep the writeup as a portfolio piece.

## Verdict

**Kill the paid diligence report. Run the free benchmark once, and treat the co-op as the real asset.**

This idea does better than its parent (r2, 29→36) on the one axis the verifier flagged — **the money is unambiguously there.** CodeDD will sell a PE firm a codebase audit tomorrow for €4,490 with a self-serve LoC calculator, Black Duck runs an audit hotline out of Burlington for "thousands" of M&A deals, and Sema signed "7 of the 9 largest global investors." No amount of skepticism makes that budget imaginary. The r2 verification pass was right that this buyer was never tested and should have been.

It also fails, for three reasons that are each independently sufficient and none of which are about market size:

1. **The question is not being asked.** Sema predicted in March 2024 that "GenAI will become a key part of technical due diligence." In February 2026 — twenty-three months later — Black Duck published a four-pillar framework titled "Evaluating AI powered applications: A new frontier for M&A software due diligence," and **AI code authorship is not one of the four pillars, or a sub-point of any of them.** The entire document is about AI in the *product*. Crosslake's "AI diligence" line is likewise about product defensibility. Silverthread is the sole incumbent framing on human-vs-AI code health, and they got there before Alex with DoD references. Meanwhile the company that owned the thesis stopped writing about it in October 2024 and now sells a Slack-search agent. Two and a half years of runway and the demand never showed up in the deliverable.
2. **The evidence base is defeatable, and the defeat is shipped by the vendor.** The product reads git trailers. Claude Code ships a mode whose system prompt says *"NEVER include ... Co-Authored-By lines or any other attribution."* A seller with any incentive to look human runs one history rewrite and the report reads 0%. Black Duck's snippet matching against a petabyte KnowledgeBase is immune to that because it reads the code itself — and it answers the question with actual legal consequence, which is licence contamination, not authorship. **A diligence method that only works on honest sellers is not a diligence method.**
3. **The liability structure excludes the principal.** This is a business where a third party makes a nine-figure decision partly on your document. That is exactly why the incumbents are insured entities with certification programs and thousands of prior engagements. A junior with $1,000 and no E&O cannot responsibly issue that opinion, and — the harder fact — will not be given the target's source code to form it. The first sale requires the credential, and the credential requires the first sale.

**What is actually worth doing, ranked.**

- **The free public benchmark, once, in a weekend.** Run the forensic pass over public repos and publish the numbers: agent-trailer prevalence 2024→2026, review hygiene on agent-trailed PRs vs human PRs, test discrimination on both. No NDA, no client, no liability, no insurance. It is a genuinely novel dataset, it is citable, and it is the only artifact that gets a diligence shop to return a student's email. If it produces zero inbound in 60 days, KC6 closes the file at a cost of one weekend.
- **The Northeastern co-op, seriously.** Black Duck is in Burlington, 20 minutes from campus, and runs the audit business this dossier just spent 90 minutes mapping. A co-op there puts Alex inside the buying process, on the insured side of the liability line, with access to the corpus that is the actual moat. That is a far better return on this research than $9,000 of one-time revenue with deal-level exposure.
- **KC1 and KC3 before anything else.** Three conversations and one insurance quote. If the practitioners say the question never comes up and the E&O quote exceeds two engagements' revenue, this file closes for under two hours of work.

**And the transferable finding, which is the most valuable output here:** the r2 dossier found loud pain with no budget; this one found loud budget with no pain. Both fail. The pattern across both is that Alex's provenance primitives keep landing next to money that is being spent on a *different question* — licence exposure, review throughput — and never on provenance itself. That is now two independent tests of the same thesis, and the thesis should be considered answered.
